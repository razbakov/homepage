## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Ten years. I have lived in Munich for ten years, and I still cannot drive a car. I still do not have a German certificate. Both have been on the list the entire time. Turns out you can build thirty products and still avoid the two things you actually said you wanted.

## Point 1 — The trick avoidance plays (0:20–1:10)

Here is the thing nobody tells you about avoidance. It does not feel like avoidance. It feels like priorities. There is always something more urgent. A launch. A feature. A partner waiting. And you keep moving — hard — in every direction. You are not lazy. You are not stuck. You are shipping. You are building. You are productive by every metric that matters. Except the two that actually matter to you. You can cross a hundred thresholds and still be standing in front of the same two doors you have never walked through. That is the trick. Avoidance dressed up as ambition looks exactly like progress.

## Point 2 — The comfortable thresholds (1:10–2:10)

Let me be honest about why it has been so easy to skip. You do not need a Führerschein if you live in central Munich. U-Bahn, S-Bahn, trains — they go everywhere. You do not need a German certificate if your job is in English, your friends are international, your whole professional world runs in English. You can build an entire life here — a real one, a full one — without ever crossing those two lines. And I did. For a decade. But then you are sitting across from a landlord, or a doctor, and something actually matters — and you feel the gap. Or you are looking at a car — a Tesla, one of those Chinese EVs flooding the market — and you realize: I literally cannot drive it. Both blocks are ten years old. Both keep getting deferred. Both just sit on the list, quietly reminding you of who you said you would become.

`[B-ROLL: Munich U-Bahn map, a conversation in German where someone hesitates, a car key sitting untouched on a table]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "You can build a whole life without crossing those two lines."]`

## Point 3 — The commitment (2:10–3:05)

So this year — 2026 — I am doing both. The Goethe B1 certificate and the German driver's license. In parallel. Not one and then the other. Both. My friend Kurt and I have been doing weekly German lessons. This morning we tried our first Goethe listening test. Next Sunday, conversational. I am booking the exam this week. I have not signed up for a Fahrschule yet — that is the next step, by the end of this month. And I am saying it here, publicly, because I want it to be expensive to back out. If you have been in Germany for five, eight, ten years and the same two boxes are unchecked — I want to hear from you. There is something useful in admitting it out loud with someone who has been carrying the same weight.
