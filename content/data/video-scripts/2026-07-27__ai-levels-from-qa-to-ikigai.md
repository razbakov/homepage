# Daily — What Level of AI Are You On? From Q&A to Ikigai

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-04-11-ai-levels-from-qa-to-ikigai

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Most people use AI like a search engine. Ask a question, get an answer, move on. That's Level 1. There are nine more levels after that — and the highest one has nothing to do with technology at all.

## Point 1 — The Ladder (0:20–1:10)

So here's the framework. I think about AI mastery like a corporate hierarchy. Level 1, you ask questions. Level 2, you give tasks — "write this email," "summarize this." Level 3, you become a manager. You stop giving steps and start defining outcomes. You chain prompts. You use agents. Levels 4 through 6, you keep climbing — prioritizing across projects, setting strategy, setting vision. By Level 6 you're the CEO of your own AI system. Everything runs beneath you. But here's where it gets interesting. Level 7, you become the board. You evaluate yourself. You govern your own system. Level 8, you're an investor — portfolio thinking across multiple systems. And then Level 9. Ikigai. That's the one that matters. The point isn't to sprint to the top. The point is to know which step you're standing on right now.

## Point 2 — The Anatomy of an Agent (1:10–2:10)

Now, to move up those levels, you need to understand what an AI agent actually is. Four parts. First — a system prompt. That's the identity. The role, the responsibilities, the guardrails. Second — the model. Claude thinks differently than GPT. Pick the right brain for the job. Third — skills. Reusable prompts. Recipes the agent can follow. And fourth — tools. Including the ability to launch other agents. That's recursion. An agent that can run agents is a fundamentally different thing than an agent that just answers questions. This is why I tell everyone — if you want to understand where AI is going, learn recursion. Take CS50. It's free. That one concept unlocks the entire agentic paradigm. Without it, you're stuck managing tools. With it, you're building systems.

`[B-ROLL: Screen recording of agents spawning sub-agents in a terminal, tasks cascading]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "An agent that launches agents — that's the unlock."]`

## Point 3 — The Ikigai Layer (2:10–3:05)

But here's what I keep coming back to. Level 9 — Ikigai — isn't about AI at all. It's the Japanese idea that your best life sits at the intersection of four things: what you love, what the world needs, what you can be paid for, and what you're good at. I think of it as the tension between the Heart — the child inside you that wants — and the Head — the parent that executes. Great work happens when both are aligned. And Level 10? I call it the Quasi World. That's the flow state. The waterfall of thoughts that arrives before you can even articulate them. AI doesn't create that. But it's the first tool fast enough to capture it. That's why the levels matter. You climb so the technology stops being the bottleneck — and you become the bottleneck. That's when the real work begins.
