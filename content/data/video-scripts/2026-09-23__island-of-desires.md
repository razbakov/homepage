## Hook (0:00–0:20)

You know that feeling — zero motivation, zero desire, like the whole world went grey? I used to think something was wrong with me when that happened. It's not broken. It's just — you're underwater. And you forgot which way is up.

## Point 1 — The Ocean (0:20–1:10)

Here's the metaphor I keep coming back to. Life is an ocean. Sometimes you're on the surface, breathing, seeing the horizon. Sometimes you're deep under — pressure from every side, no light, no air. Just weight. That underwater place? That's apathy. That's the weeks where every day tastes the same. You can't see the island. You can't even see the sky. And the longer you stay down there, the more you start to believe the surface doesn't exist. That maybe your island was never real. But here's the thing — something always drags you back up. A crisis. A shock. A shark swims by and suddenly you're fighting again. You break the surface and you gasp. And for one second — you can see it. Your island. Maybe it's a mirage. Maybe it's real. But you see it.

`[B-ROLL: slow-motion ocean surface from below, light breaking through waves]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Something always drags you back up."]`

## Point 2 — The Compass (1:10–2:10)

That moment on the surface — that breath — it's everything. Because that's when you set your direction. You look at the star. You feel the current. And you choose. Most people waste that moment. They just breathe and sink again. But if you lock in, if you fix your eyes on the island and actually pick a heading — now you have a compass. Now the underwater parts aren't aimless anymore. You're still swimming. You still feel the pressure. But you know where you're going. And the current — your environment, the people around you — it either carries you toward the island or pushes you away. If it pushes, you don't fight the ocean. You find a different current. Or a different island entirely. There's no honour in drowning for the wrong destination.

`[B-ROLL: compass spinning, then settling on a direction; aerial shot of ocean current patterns]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Fix the direction. Then swim."]`

## Point 3 — The Stops Along the Way (2:10–3:05)

One more thing I had to learn the hard way. The island isn't the only place worth being. There are reefs along the way — beautiful ones. There are smaller islands where you rest and catch your breath. Continents you never planned to visit. Don't rush past them. Some of what you're chasing — you can actually have it today. Right now. A piece of the dream, already here, just smaller than you imagined. Stop. Look at the reef. Enjoy the fish. Then keep swimming. And find other swimmers heading your way. You don't cross an ocean alone. You cross it with people who share your current.
