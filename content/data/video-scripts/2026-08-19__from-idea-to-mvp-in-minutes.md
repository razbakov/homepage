## Point 1 — Structure before code (0:20–1:10)

Here's what normally happens. You get an idea, you get excited, you tell an AI: "Build me a trading bot." And it does. You get code. Maybe even good code. But you skipped the thinking. You skipped *why this idea*, *for whom*, *what's the hypothesis*. You jumped straight to the answer without asking the question.

So I built a skill — a single markdown file — that forces you through design thinking before a single line of code gets written. Discovery. Hypothesis framing. Validation planning. Then prototyping. It asks you hard questions. Who is this for? What does success look like? What's the biggest obstacle? You answer. It synthesizes. And *then* it builds.

On that stream, it produced a README, a product strategy, user personas, an architecture plan, and three prototype approaches. One session. No blank-page paralysis.

## Point 2 — The estimation illusion (1:10–2:10)

Now here's where it gets funny. The skill estimates timelines. It'll say: "This prototype will take two to three weeks." It takes five minutes. Five.

Why? Because the AI learned from human data. Before agents could write code, a trading bot prototype *really did* take weeks. The AI is pattern-matching on a world that no longer exists. The timeline compressed by orders of magnitude, but the model hasn't caught up.

My workaround is story points. Forget hours. Work with AI agents for a week, calibrate your velocity, and then you have a conversion rate that actually reflects reality. But this tells you something deeper — we are all still thinking in old timelines. We budget weeks for things that take minutes. And that mental lag? That's the real bottleneck. Not the technology.

## Point 3 — Ideas stop being scary (2:10–3:05)

I've used this workflow to bootstrap fifteen-plus projects. Most of them? Killed after the prototype phase. A few survived. And that's exactly the point.

When exploring an idea costs you five minutes of structured thinking and five minutes of building, ideas stop being precious. You don't need to *commit* to find out. You don't need a weekend. You don't need permission. You prototype, you react to something concrete instead of debating something abstract, and you decide: proceed, pivot, or kill.

Fail fast. Fail cheap. Keep the winners. That's not a slogan — that's Tuesday now.
