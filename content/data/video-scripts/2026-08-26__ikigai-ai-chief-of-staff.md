## Point 1 — The question I was avoiding (0:20–1:10)

I'm a software engineer. I build things for a living. But outside of work, I had five projects running at the same time — a dance app, a budgeting tool, an online academy, an AI study group, teaching salsa on Sundays. None of them made money. None of them were validated. And I couldn't tell you which one mattered.

So I sat down with Claude Code and it asked me something I wasn't ready for. Not "what's your roadmap?" Not "who's your user?" It asked: why does this need to exist at all?

And I typed something I'd never said out loud. "This project is about why I exist. This is my ikigai."

I'd been telling people I was building apps. But what I was actually doing — for years — was searching for meaning. The AI didn't give me that answer. It just made me stop hiding from it.

## Point 2 — One sentence, one bet (1:10–2:10)

Then the AI did something I couldn't do for myself. It took everything — the five projects, the full-time job, the desire for independence — and compressed it into a single hypothesis: find the one project people will actually pay for, and go all in.

Not "build all five." Not "quit your job tomorrow." Just — validate one thing. That's the whole bet.

And right after that clicked, I realized I already had a test. A salsa festival in Vienna. Fifty dancers. A partner with half a million followers on social media. And one question: will someone pay one euro for what I built?

Suddenly I had something I'd never had with my side projects — a real deadline, a real audience, and real skin in the game. I'd been treating these projects like hobbies. No accountability. No pressure. No data. That ended.

## Point 3 — The system behind the search (2:10–3:05)

But here's the part most people skip. Having clarity is not the same as having follow-through. So the AI proposed something simple — not a productivity system, not a framework with twelve pillars. Three rituals. A daily check-in at nine AM: what matters today? A weekly review on Saturday: what actually happened? And weekly planning right after: what's next?

That's it. Three moments where something asks you — are you working on what matters?

I set them up in my calendar that same session. And I connected two AI systems — one for deep work, one for nudges and reminders — both reading from the same source of truth. One life operating system. Not because the technology is impressive, but because I know myself. Without the structure, I drift. With it, I test.
