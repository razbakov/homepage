# Daily — How to Become a Graphic Designer

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2018-08-20-how-to-become-a-graphic-designer

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Nobody taught me design. I just… started asking the right questions. And that's the thing — becoming a graphic designer isn't about talent. It's about four honest questions and the willingness to follow where they lead.

## Point 1 — The Four Questions (0:20–1:10)

Before you open Figma, before you pick a course, before you do anything — sit down and answer four things. Why do I want this job? What kind of company do I actually want to work for? Where are those jobs posted? And — this is the hard one — do I already have the skills, or am I pretending I do?

Most people skip this. They jump straight into tutorials, collect certificates, build a portfolio of stuff nobody asked for. But if you don't know *why* you're doing this, you'll design for nobody. You'll aim at nothing and hit it perfectly.

These four questions are boring. They're not sexy. But they're the difference between someone who becomes a designer and someone who just talks about it.

`[B-ROLL: Notebook with handwritten questions, coffee shop setting]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Why do I want this job?"]`

## Point 2 — Learn the Fundamentals, Not the Tools (1:10–2:10)

Here's where people go wrong. They think becoming a designer means learning Photoshop. No. It means learning *design*. Typography. Grids. Hierarchy. How letters breathe on a page. How spacing creates trust before a single word is read.

You can learn this without design school. There are incredible free resources — guides on harmonious typography, on how grids actually work in digital design, on the basics that separate amateurs from professionals. I link to the best ones in the full essay.

The tool is just a tool. Figma, Sketch, Illustrator — they change every few years. But the principles? Typography and spacing and visual rhythm? Those are centuries old. Learn what doesn't expire.

`[B-ROLL: Split screen — messy amateur layout vs. clean professional layout with the same content]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Learn what doesn't expire."]`

## Point 3 — Organize Like a Pro Before You Are One (2:10–3:05)

This one surprised me. One of the biggest tells of an amateur designer isn't their work — it's their files. Folders named "final_v3_FINAL_real." Layers called "Layer 47 copy copy." It's chaos.

Professionals organize. They have filing systems. They name things. They keep their design files clean so that when a client comes back six months later, they can actually find the project.

Start this habit now — before you have clients, before you have a portfolio. Because organization isn't a skill you add later. It's how you think. And how you think is what separates a designer from someone who just makes things look nice.
