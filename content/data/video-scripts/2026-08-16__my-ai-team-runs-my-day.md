## Point 1 — The morning I didn't plan (0:20–1:10)

Every morning at nine, Maya — my AI chief of staff — sends me a Telegram message. "Good morning. Here's your schedule." Meetings, deep work blocks, lunch, dinner, even train tickets. All planned. I didn't touch any of it. The night before, she checked my calendar, scanned my task board in Notion, looked at what's in the kitchen, and built the whole day. I just wake up and follow it. Now — each agent gets a fifteen-minute slot. Kai asks about my community strategy. Marco pushes on a business partnership. Viktor reviews open pull requests. These aren't vague check-ins. Each one knows their domain. They ask hard, specific questions. It's like having a small team that actually prepared for the meeting.

`[B-ROLL: Split screen — Notion kanban board on one side, calendar with color-coded agent meetings on the other]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "6 agents. 15-minute meetings. Zero status updates."]`

## Point 2 — Execute vs. question (1:10–2:10)

Here's the part that surprised me. Plans change, right? So midday I message Maya: "Skip dinner, I'm doing shisha with a friend instead." She updates the calendar, adjusts the evening, confirms. Done. No friction. But — if I send that same message to Sage, my coaching agent, I get a completely different response. She doesn't just comply. She asks why. Is it stress? Am I avoiding something? That's the real power of having multiple agents. Maya executes. Sage questions. One keeps the train moving. The other makes sure it's going somewhere worth going. You need both. And a single AI can't do both well, because compliance and challenge are opposite postures. So you split them.

`[B-ROLL: Two chat windows side by side — Maya's quick confirmation vs. Sage's probing follow-up question]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Maya executes. Sage questions."]`

## Point 3 — The system underneath (2:10–3:05)

This isn't hacked together. There are three frameworks baked into the agents' behavior. GTD — everything hits an inbox, gets clarified, becomes a task. Sociocracy — clear domains, consent-based decisions. Design thinking — discovery before building. When Marco evaluates a new idea, he actually follows discovery phases. When Maya triages my inbox, she applies GTD classification. The whole thing runs on Claude Code, Telegram bots, Notion, and Google Calendar. No SaaS platform. No subscription beyond API costs. Before this, I context-switched all day. Start coding, remember an email, forget lunch, end the day feeling busy but empty. Now I make decisions. That's it. The agents handle planning, scheduling, drafting, researching — even posting. The only thing I can't delegate is showing up.
