# Daily — Your Emotions Are Not Yours

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-07-30-your-emotions-are-not-yours

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Your emotions are not yours. I know — sounds wrong. You feel them in your chest, behind your eyes, in your throat. Of course they're yours. But that possessive — "my anger," "my fear" — might be the quietest and most expensive mistake you ever make.

## Point 1 — Weather, not factory (0:20–1:10)

We all carry this hidden assumption: something happens, and I *produce* a feeling. Like a factory. Raw material in, finished emotion out. My product, made on my premises.

But watch a feeling actually show up. You don't *decide* to be afraid. Fear is already there before you've even registered what scared you. Grief doesn't arrive on schedule — it walks in and sits down, uninvited, at the worst possible hour.

Nothing about that resembles production. It resembles weather. A front moves in. The light changes. You didn't make the storm — you're standing in it.

That's the reframe. A feeling isn't something you generate and own. It's emotional weather, showing up locally — in you. Like a man on a hillside saying "my wind." The wind is bending his coat, stinging his eyes. But he didn't make it. It didn't begin at his skin. And it'll still be moving across the valley long after he goes inside.

`[TEXT ON SCREEN: You didn't make the storm. You're standing in it.]`

## Point 2 — The cost of ownership (1:10–2:10)

Now — this would just be a nice metaphor if it didn't matter. But I think mistaking weather for private property is the root of most of the suffering a feeling causes. And that's different from the feeling itself.

Think about what ownership forces you to do. If the fear is *mine,* it's a verdict on me. Evidence of weakness. Something I need to hide or fix. If the rage is *mine,* I either act it out or choke it down — because a possession demands a decision. And if the grief is mine and mine alone? Then I'm sealed in a room no one else has ever entered. Completely alone inside it.

Every one of those torments comes not from the feeling — but from the deed to it. Drop the deed, and the feeling stays, but the second suffering falls away. The fear isn't a verdict anymore. It's weather. And weather is not about you. The grief — the grief is actually the *least* private thing you'll ever feel. It's the same thing that's moved through every human who ever lost someone, arriving now at your window.

`[B-ROLL: Rain on a window, slowly pulling back to reveal an open landscape beyond]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Drop the deed. The second suffering falls away.]`

## Point 3 — Standing in it (2:10–3:05)

So what does mastery actually mean? We hear "master your emotions" and we picture control. Sitting on the lid. Tighter grip. Less escaping.

But you can't own the weather harder. There's no lid. The field runs through you whether you consent or not.

Mastery, in this picture, is something else entirely. It's learning to stand consciously in a field that runs through everyone. To feel the front move in, know it for what it is — not mine, not a verdict, not an emergency — and keep choosing your direction while it passes, instead of being blown flat.

The wind still blows. The difference is whether you think you *are* the wind — or whether you're the one who can stay standing in it. Coat bent. Eyes stinging. Still walking the way you meant to walk. That's not a colder life. It's a vastly less lonely one.
