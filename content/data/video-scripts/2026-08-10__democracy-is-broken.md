# Daily — Democracy Is Broken. Here's an Architecture to Fix It.

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-04-07-democracy-is-broken

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Democracy is broken. Not controversial — obvious. You vote for strangers you've never met, based on speeches you half-watched, and call that governance. I think I found an architecture that actually fixes it — by combining three ideas nobody's connected before.

## Point 1 — Liquid Democracy (0:20–1:10)

Here's what's always bothered me. Every election, I'm asked to pick between people I don't know. Meanwhile — I know a doctor. I know an engineer. I know a teacher. People whose judgment I've watched for years, in real situations.

What if I could give my healthcare vote to my doctor friend? My infrastructure vote to my engineer? And they do the same for the stuff they're not experts in. That's liquid democracy. You vote directly — or you delegate to someone you trust. They can delegate further. And you can take it back anytime.

Democracy Earth built a protocol for this back in 2017. Y Combinator backed it. Then it went dormant. But the idea? The idea is still the most powerful upgrade to democracy I've seen.

The problem is participation. Nobody has time to think about two hundred decisions a year. And that's where the second idea comes in.

`[B-ROLL: hands passing a glowing token along a chain of people in a circle]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Delegate to people you actually trust."]`

## Point 2 — Your AI Knows Your Values (1:10–2:10)

What if you had an AI agent that knew your values? Not your opinion on some policy — your actual values. The things you'd choose if you had unlimited time and information.

That agent votes on your behalf when you're busy. Delegates to your trusted circle when the topic is outside your range. Alerts you when something important needs your personal call.

That's what I'm building with Ikigai Team. It starts with a coaching session — an AI coach helps you reflect on what matters to you, scores ten areas of your life, finds your direction. Then your AI team is calibrated to those values.

Right now it's a personal productivity tool. Six agents managing your day. But underneath — it's a governance prototype. And this connects to a third idea that blew my mind.

`[B-ROLL: person in conversation with an AI interface, reflective, calm lighting]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "An AI calibrated to your values — not your opinions."]`

## Point 3 — Three Layers (2:10–3:05)

Jacque Fresco asked: what if we managed resources directly through technology, no money at all? Sensors, AI, allocation by need. Beautiful vision. But his blind spot is governance — he says scientists will figure it out. No. Someone has to define what "the common good" means. That's not science. That's values.

So here's the architecture. Three layers. Layer one: resource management — sensors and AI handle logistics. Objective, measurable. Layer two: values and direction — liquid democracy. Humans decide what we optimize for. You delegate to people you trust or to your AI agent. Layer three: personal sovereignty — your AI team is your interface to the whole system. It knows your values because it started with coaching.

One thread holds it all together: radical openness. Open code. Open protocols. Open governance. Because closed systems cannot be trusted with power. Period.
