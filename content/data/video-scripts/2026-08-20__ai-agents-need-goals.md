# Daily — AI Agents Need Goals Too: How I Gave My Bots OKRs

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-03-25-ai-agents-need-goals

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

My AI agents were lazy. Six of them — sitting in Telegram, doing nothing unless I told them exactly what to do. Same energy as a team with no direction. Then I tried something simple. I gave them OKRs. And they started acting like they actually cared.

## Point 1 — The reactive trap (0:20–1:10)

Here's the thing about most AI setups. You ask, it answers. You instruct, it executes. It's a tool sitting on a shelf until you pick it up. That works fine when you have one assistant. But I have six agents across different domains — operations, engineering, content, strategy, coaching, community. And when none of them know what they're working toward, they just mirror your last instruction. No one thinks ahead. No one flags a problem before it hits you. It's the exact same failure mode as a human team with no goals. They show up, they do what's asked, they clock out. Zero ownership.

`[B-ROLL: Assembly line of identical robot arms, idle, waiting]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: No goals = no initiative]`

## Point 2 — One message changed everything (1:10–2:10)

So I sent one message to the team chat. "Every agent needs to define their own OKRs connected to mine." Within minutes — minutes — each agent came back with proposed quarterly objectives. Maya, my chief of staff, committed to daily check-ins five times a week and reducing my context-switching by half. Viktor, the CTO, locked onto shipping the payment flow and maintaining zero critical bugs. Luna proposed three blog posts a week. Marco said he'd validate pricing with fifty paying users. They cascaded from my goals into their own domains. I didn't assign these. They drafted them. And that shift — from me pushing tasks to them pulling objectives — that's the whole point. The agents went from "what should I do?" to "here's what I think we should do."

`[B-ROLL: Notion board with agent OKRs, each card color-coded by agent]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "What should I do?" → "Here's what I think we should do."]`

## Point 3 — This isn't an AI trick (2:10–3:05)

Now — this isn't some clever prompt hack. This is a management principle. Clear goals, shared accountability, measurable outcomes. It's what makes human teams effective. Turns out it makes AI teams effective too. Maya sends morning briefings now because daily check-ins are her OKR. Luna pitches content topics because she's tracking publication frequency. Marco pushes festival prep because paid users is his number. They suggest tasks. They flag risks. They prioritize without me. The OKRs live in the project README — visible to every agent in every conversation. Persistent context that shapes behavior. That's the difference between a useful chatbot and an actual team member. Direction.
