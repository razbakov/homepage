# Daily — You Are a Neuron in a Larger Mind

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-07-30-you-are-a-neuron-in-a-larger-mind

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Right now, a single cell in your brain is part of the thought you're having. It has no idea. It can't know. It just fires — yes or no — and passes it along. And yet the thought is real, and that cell is genuinely inside it. Here's what I can't stop thinking: you are that neuron. To something larger. And sitting with that honestly changes everything.

## Point 1 — You were never the author (0:20–1:10)

We build our whole identity around authorship. My thoughts. My achievements. My significance. As if each of us is a sealed source, generating meaning from the inside and pushing it outward.

But look at the neuron. It contributes something indispensable to the thought — remove enough cells like it and the thought degrades. And yet it is in no sense the *author* of that thought. The authorship lives in the pattern. In the relationship between that cell and everything it's wired to.

So if you're a cell in a larger mind, your significance was never yours to generate. It was always relational. It only exists in the firing-together — in the connection to everyone around you. That's why the lonely, sealed-off version of a meaningful life never quite works. You were trying to be the source of a value that only ever appears *between*.

`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Significance was never yours to generate.]`

## Point 2 — Indispensable, not central (1:10–2:10)

Now you could hear all that as a demotion. Just a cell. One of a hundred billion. Interchangeable.

But watch what the neuron actually is in the moment it fires. The thought cannot happen without it. It's not a spare. It's not waiting in reserve. The whole depends, right now, on this exact cell doing its exact small thing. And at the same time — that cell is not the seat of the whole. It doesn't run the mind. It can't even see the mind.

Both are true at once. And living inside both at once is, I think, the most honest posture available to us. Fully committed. Entirely un-grandiose. Most of our suffering comes from getting this backwards — acting central when we're indispensable, or feeling worthless when we're simply not central. The neuron has it right.

`[B-ROLL: single musician playing in an orchestra — camera pulls back slowly to reveal the full ensemble]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Indispensable without being central.]`

## Point 3 — Death changes shape (2:10–3:05)

Here's where it does its hardest work.

We fear death as deletion. Total erasure of our inner point of view. And if you really are a sealed source — yeah, that fear is exactly right. When you go, the author is gone. The work goes dark.

But neurons die constantly throughout your life, and the thoughts they helped shape don't die with them. What the cell contributed — the connections it strengthened, the patterns it helped lay down — stays in the network. It was never stored inside that one cell. It lived in the relationships.

I'm not promising you survive. I don't know what's on the other side. But the fear of *total* deletion is aimed at a self the model says you never were. The neuron ends. The thought it helped think goes on. And you were always, only ever, the neuron.
