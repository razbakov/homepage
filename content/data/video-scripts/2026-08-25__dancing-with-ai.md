## Point 1 — Muscle Memory (0:20–1:10)

Here's why I believe this. I've been teaching Cuban salsa for years. On the dance floor, you don't think about your steps. Your body just *knows*. Muscle memory takes over — you feel the music, you feel your partner, and the right move happens without you deciding it.

Now imagine that same thing, but for work. You're standing in a virtual workspace. Every project lives somewhere in the space around you. Step forward — that's your current task. Quick move to the right — different project. No file trees. No tab switching. Your body remembers the layout the same way a dancer remembers a routine. Not in your head. In your legs, in your arms, in your spine.

It's choreography for knowledge work.

## Point 2 — Prompts Are Spells (1:10–2:10)

Now layer AI on top of this. You speak, and an interface appears — right there, floating in front of you at that spot in space. Walk somewhere else, speak again, different tool shows up. Nothing is pre-built. Everything appears on demand through conversation.

And here's where it clicks for me. Prompts are spells. You speak — and something materializes. A dashboard. A draft. A data visualization. These are artifacts, conjured through language and shaped through movement. Your workspace becomes a spell book, and every corner holds something you've summoned before.

We speak faster than we type. We move faster than we speak. And we dance faster than all of it. Typing is also a kind of dancing, if you think about it — fingers in learned patterns across keys. Vim users know this. But it's dancing constrained to a tiny surface. VR removes that constraint. Your whole body becomes the input device.

Picture this. You walk into a room and all your projects are floating around you. WeDance over here, a blog post draft over there, a code review behind you.
