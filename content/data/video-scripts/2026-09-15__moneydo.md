## Point 1 — The Trap of Over-Budgeting (0:20–1:10)

Here's what nobody tells you about budgeting. The "right" way — fifty categories, track every coffee, color-coded spreadsheets — it's a trap. You start strong. Two weeks in, something unplanned hits. You miss a day of tracking. Then guilt kicks in, you stop looking at the numbers altogether, and you're back to guessing.

I lived that loop for a decade. Every new app, every new system — same ending. And the worst part? I could never answer the simplest question: can I buy new jeans? Like, genuinely — I had no idea. My budget said one thing, my bank account said another, and my gut said just don't look.

The problem was never discipline. The problem was complexity.

## Point 2 — Four Rules That Actually Work (1:10–2:10)

So here's the method that changed it. Money comes in — any amount — you split it immediately. Ten to twenty percent goes to savings, straight to a separate account. Don't think about it. Half goes to needs — rent, groceries, medicine. Everything left is wants.

Then — and this is the key — you set a daily limit. Not a monthly budget you forget about. A daily number. If you don't spend it today, it rolls to tomorrow. So big purchases naturally drift to the end of the month, when you've stacked up enough.

You only need four envelopes: Needs, Wants, Culture, Extra. That's it. No tracking every transaction. You only track the irregular stuff — the surprises — so you can spot patterns and change them.

And when you mess up? You re-plan. You don't spiral. You look at what's left, adjust, and keep going. Stay alert, not anxious. That one line rewired how I think about money.

## Point 3 — Building MoneyDo (2:10–3:05)

This worked so well that I built an app around it. MoneyDo. Because every budgeting app I'd tried was designed for the old method — the one that kept failing me. They want you to categorize everything. They need five clicks to log a coffee.

MoneyDo calculates your daily limit for you. Adding an expense is three taps. You can drag to re-adjust your budget in seconds. It works offline, installs in ten seconds, runs on desktop and mobile. Three euros a month.

But honestly — the app is just the vehicle. The method is the thing. Whether you're a freelancer with unpredictable income or someone on a steady salary, it works because it's forgiving. It doesn't punish you for being human.
