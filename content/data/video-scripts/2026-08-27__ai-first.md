# Daily — AI Secretary: What If You Let AI Tell You What to Do Next?

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2025-01-10-ai-first

I stopped managing my tasks. I let AI do it instead. Not a chatbot, not another Kanban board — I built a team of AI experts that live inside my code editor and tell me what to work on next. And honestly? I don't think I can go back.

## Point 1 — The Problem with Backlogs (0:20–1:10)

Here's what kept happening. I'd set up a ticketing system — Jira, Linear, Notion, take your pick — and for a week it'd feel great. Organized. Clean columns. Then life happens. The backlog grows. The board goes stale. And I end up doing whatever feels urgent anyway, ignoring the whole system I spent hours building.

The real issue? I was spending more energy *organizing* work than *doing* work. So I flipped the question. What if instead of me telling the system what needs to happen… the system tells me?

That's AI Secretary. Six AI experts that know my projects inside out — my goals, my decisions, my code, my docs — and they surface what actually matters *right now*. Not what I wrote down three weeks ago.

`[B-ROLL: Split screen — messy Kanban board on left, clean IDE conversation on right]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Organizing work ≠ doing work"]`

## Point 2 — Six Experts, Zero Tickets (1:10–2:10)

I don't have a project manager. I have perspectives. Six of them. A personal secretary that runs my daily check-ins. A business expert for "is this worth my time?" A product expert for architecture gut-checks. A documentation manager that keeps docs alive without me thinking about it. And a couple more.

The key insight — none of this lives in a separate app. It's all inside my IDE, reading the same files I'm working in. So when I sit down in the morning, the AI doesn't show me a frozen snapshot of some planning meeting. It looks at the *current state* of the code, the docs, the decisions — and tells me what moves the needle today.

No backlog. No sprints. No board with columns. Just: here's what matters, based on everything that's true right now.

`[B-ROLL: Cursor IDE with different AI "expert" conversations, quick cuts between them]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "No backlog. No sprints. Just what matters now."]`

## Point 3 — The Rules That Make It Work (2:10–3:05)

This didn't work on day one. It took months to figure out the rules. I'll give you the big ones. One task, one conversation — keep context small or the AI loses the thread. Documentation first — the AI needs to understand *why* before it can help with *how*. Commit after every small win so you can experiment freely. And my favorite: build your own language. Short commands instead of long prompts. Over time, you and the AI develop a shared vocabulary that makes everything faster.

I'm a solo developer with too many ideas and not enough hours. The question I keep asking is: am I working on what matters? A ticket board can't answer that. But an AI that knows your goals, your constraints, and your code — that actually can.
