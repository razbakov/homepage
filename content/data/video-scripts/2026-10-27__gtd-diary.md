## Hook (0:00–0:20)
Paper beats your phone. I know — sounds ridiculous. But after years of bouncing between apps and notebooks, I keep coming back to a folded piece of paper in my pocket. And today I want to show you why a printable template might be the most underrated productivity tool you'll ever use.

## Point 1 — The Template That Hooked Me (0:20–1:10)
Years ago I stumbled on Dave Seah's Printable CEO templates online. Dave runs a design studio, and instead of using some fancy project management tool, he hands his team these paper sheets. Simple. Printed. Done. I translated a few into Russian, tweaked them for myself, and something clicked. The daily template does three things: you pick your key tasks for the day, you lay out a schedule that accounts for meetings and breaks, and you capture every small thought that pops up as you go. That's it. No notifications. No syncing. No "which app do I open." You write it down, you see it, you do it. There's a clarity that comes from a pen touching paper that no screen has ever given me.

`[B-ROLL: Close-up of the printed daily planner template being filled in with a pen — task list on the left, time blocks on the right]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Pick tasks. Schedule blocks. Capture thoughts."]`

## Point 2 — Evolving the System (1:10–2:10)
But here's where it got interesting. I didn't stop at the original. I built a project sheet — one page where you break a big goal into subtasks with deadlines. Then a timing sheet, because if you want to manage your time, you first need to see where it actually goes. And then I redesigned the whole thing. I added a notes block, a section for "time wasters" and "accelerators" — things that slow you down versus things that speed you up. I added a "defer" block for stuff that doesn't belong today. And on the back: contacts, a mind map of the day, income and expenses. Two pages, printed double-sided, folded down to A6. Fits in your pocket. Your entire day — planned, tracked, reviewed — in something smaller than your phone. Eventually I even simplified it further: just a grid sheet with a time column. Because the best system is the one simple enough that you actually use it.

`[B-ROLL: Montage of the different template versions — project list, timing sheet, the colorful "day control" form, the minimalist grid sheet — laid out on a desk]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "The best system is the one you actually use."]`

## Point 3 — The Real Tension (2:10–3:05)
Now, I'll be honest. I still haven't settled. For years I've been jumping back and forth — phone, paper, phone, paper. And I think that tension is the point. Digital is great for search, for sync, for reminders. But paper forces you to choose. You can't write down twenty tasks on a small sheet — you have to pick the three that matter. That constraint is a feature. A weekly review sheet where you score each task in points and see your productivity trend — that's not busywork, that's self-awareness. The question isn't "paper or digital." The question is: does your system make you stop and think, or does it just make you feel busy?
