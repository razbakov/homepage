# Daily — Your Emotions Are Not Yours

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-07-30-your-emotions-are-not-yours

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Your emotions are not yours. I know how that sounds — of course they're yours. You feel them in your chest, behind your eyes, in the tightening of your own throat. But that little word — "my" anger, "my" fear — it might be the quietest and most expensive mistake you ever make.

## Point 1 — The Factory Story (0:20–1:10)

We all carry this default model. Something happens, I produce a feeling. Like a factory — raw material comes in, finished product goes out. The feeling is mine. I made it on my premises. My product.

But watch a feeling actually arrive. You don't decide to be afraid. Fear shows up before you've even finished registering what scared you. You don't manufacture grief on a schedule. It walks through the door and sits down uninvited, at the worst possible hour.

Nothing about that resembles production. It resembles weather. A front moves in. The light changes. You didn't make the storm — you're standing in it. And that one shift — from factory to weather — changes everything that comes after.

`[B-ROLL: Time-lapse of weather changing over a landscape — sun to clouds to rain to clearing]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: You're not making the storm. You're standing in it.]`

## Point 2 — The Cost of Owning (1:10–2:10)

Here's why this matters beyond philosophy. Mistaking the weather for private property is the root of most suffering a feeling causes — and that's a different thing from the feeling itself.

Think about what ownership forces you to do. If the fear is mine, it becomes a verdict on me. Evidence of weakness. A character flaw I need to hide or fix. If the rage is mine, I either act it out to honor it or choke it down in shame — because a possession demands a decision. And if the grief is mine and mine alone, then I'm sealed in a room no one else has ever entered. Completely alone inside it.

Every one of those torments comes not from the feeling but from the deed to it. Drop the deed. The feeling stays. But that second layer of suffering — that falls away.

`[B-ROLL: A person sitting alone in a dim room, then cut to the same person stepping outside into open air]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: The suffering comes from the deed, not the feeling.]`

## Point 3 — Standing Consciously (2:10–3:05)

This quietly changes what "mastering your emotions" means. That phrase almost always smuggles the factory back in — as if mastery means tighter control of your output. Sit on the lid. Let less escape.

But you cannot own the weather harder. There is no lid. The field runs through you whether you consent or not. And the effort to seal it only convinces you the storm is your private fault.

Mastery, in this picture, is learning to stand consciously in a field that runs through everyone. To feel the front move in and know it for what it is — not mine, not a verdict, not an emergency of the self — and to keep choosing my direction while it passes. The wind still blows. The difference is entirely in whether I think I am the wind, or whether I'm the one who stays standing in it — coat bent, eyes stinging, still walking the way I meant to walk.

That's not a colder life. It's a vastly less lonely one.
