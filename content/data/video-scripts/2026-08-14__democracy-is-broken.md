# Daily — Democracy Is Broken. Here's an Architecture to Fix It.

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-04-07-democracy-is-broken

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Every election, you vote for strangers. People you've never met, never watched make a hard call, never trusted with anything real. And somehow they end up deciding your healthcare, your kids' education, your future. I think there's a better architecture. One where you delegate your vote to people you actually know — and your AI handles the rest.

## Point 1 — Liquid Democracy (0:20–1:10)

Here's the thought I keep coming back to. I know a doctor. I know an engineer. I know a teacher. I've watched them think. I've seen their judgment under pressure. I trust them — not because of a campaign ad, but because I've known them for years. So what if I could give my healthcare vote to my doctor friend? My infrastructure vote to my engineer? And they do the same for topics they're not experts in. This is called liquid democracy. You vote directly when you want to, or you delegate to someone in your circle. And you can take it back anytime. The idea's been around — Democracy Earth wrote a whole protocol for it back in 2017. But nobody solved the real problem: participation. Who has time to make two hundred decisions a year?

`[B-ROLL: Network graph connecting people, nodes lighting up as votes flow between them]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Delegate to people you trust."]`

## Point 2 — AI as Your Proxy (1:10–2:10)

That's where AI changes the game. What if you had a personal agent that knew your values — not your opinions on every policy, but the things you actually care about? It votes on your behalf when you're busy. It delegates to your trust circle when the topic is outside your range. And it pings you when something big comes up that needs your eyes. This isn't science fiction. I've been building exactly this with Ikigai Team. It starts with a coaching session — your AI helps you reflect on what matters, scores ten areas of your life, finds your direction. Then your agents are calibrated to those values. Right now it's a productivity tool. Six agents helping you manage your day. But underneath? It's a governance prototype. And then I read Jacque Fresco — the Venus Project guy — who says: skip money, let sensors and AI manage all resources directly. He's half right. Machines can handle logistics. But someone still has to decide what we optimize *for*. That's not a science question. That's a values question.

`[B-ROLL: Split screen — AI dashboard on one side, physical sensors monitoring water/energy on the other]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Values are not a science question."]`

## Point 3 — Three Layers (2:10–3:05)

So here's the architecture. Three layers. Layer one: resource management. Sensors and AI handle logistics — water, energy, food. Objective, measurable, no voting needed. Layer two: values and direction. Liquid democracy. Humans decide what we optimize for — space or oceans, freedom or efficiency. You delegate to people you trust, or to your AI agent. Layer three: personal sovereignty. Your AI team is your interface to the whole system. It knows your values because it started with coaching. It participates in layer two on your behalf. And you can override anything, anytime. I'm calling the project Agora — like the ancient Greek assembly. Citizens deciding together. But this time, with better tools. And one thing holds it all together: radical openness. Every layer, open source. Because closed systems cannot be trusted with governance. Period.
