# Daily — You Feel Coherence, Not Character

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-08-06-you-feel-coherence-not-character

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

You've already decided about someone before you've seen their face. That gut read — good person, bad person — it's real. You *are* picking something up. But you're naming it wrong. What you feel isn't character. It's coherence.

## Point 1 — The signal is real (0:20–1:10)

So here's what we actually know. Collect sweat from frightened people, give it to strangers in a lab — no context, no instructions. Their faces start making fear. Their eyes widen. They report nothing unusual. A state jumped from one body to another through a channel neither person was aware of.

Then there's physiological synchrony. Heart rate, skin conductance — they drift into alignment between people who are interacting. They predict whether two strangers will cooperate. Whether a date will go well. Add thin-slicing — thirty seconds is enough for accurate reads on trust, deception, personality.

Three channels. Chemistry, physiology, a rapid inference engine you don't operate. All real, all below awareness. The question was never *whether* you pick people up. That's settled. The question is what exactly you're picking up.

## Point 2 — Feeling comes first (1:10–2:10)

Here's where it gets interesting. We assume the brain reads a situation, fires the chemistry, then you feel something. Brain authors, body receives. Three serious neuroscientists — Solms, Damasio, Panksepp — all point the arrow the other direction. Feeling is not something consciousness produces. Feeling is what consciousness is *made of*. It's older than thinking by four billion years.

And there's a timing problem. Your brain can decode which choice you'll make up to seven seconds before you're aware of choosing. Seven seconds. By the time you're conscious of a feeling, the feeling has already happened. You weren't present at its origin. You arrive afterwards and write the account — and because you wrote it, you mistake yourself for the author.

So you feel something off a stranger. The feeling is real. But the story you tell about it? That's the late-arriving narrator doing what narrators do — making it make sense. And the sense it makes is almost always moral. Good person. Bad person.

`[TEXT ON SCREEN: You arrive after the feeling and mistake yourself for its author.]`

## Point 3 — It's structure, not morality (2:10–3:05)

I used to call it good energy and bad energy. Say that out loud and you hear what it sounds like — a claim to detect evil. Then I changed one word and everything shifted.

Not good and bad. Coherent and incoherent.

The person I read as "off" is usually someone who doesn't know what to do. They're bouncing left, then right, then left again. Something hasn't gone the way they expected and they haven't caught up. They're oscillating. They're at war with themselves. That's not a moral state. It's a structural one.

Carl Rogers named it in 1957 — congruence and incongruence. And here's the part that closes the loop: he made therapist congruence a necessary condition of change *precisely because other people perceive it*. The state is detectable. That was clinical doctrine before I was born.

So if what people register off you is coherence — the work isn't managing how you come across. It's ending the war inside. Deciding the thing you've been avoiding. Closing the gap between what you do and what you actually believe. Stop oscillating.
