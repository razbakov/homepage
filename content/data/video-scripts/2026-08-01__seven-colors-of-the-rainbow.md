# Daily — Seven Colors of the Rainbow

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-08-01-seven-colors-of-the-rainbow

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Newton lied to you. Well — not lied. But he chose seven colors for the rainbow because he wanted them to match seven musical notes. The rainbow is actually a continuous spectrum. No borders. No breaks. And here's the thing — your emotions work the same way.

`[B-ROLL: Close-up of light through a prism splitting into a spectrum on a white wall]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Your emotions are a spectrum.]`

## Point 1 — One spectrum, many names (0:20–1:10)

We love to sort things into boxes. Seven colors. Seven notes. Seven chakras. Red is survival. Green is love. Violet is unity. And it maps beautifully — I lay it all out in the essay. But the deeper point isn't the mapping. It's that we treat emotions like separate objects. Anger is one thing. Love is another. Fear lives in its own drawer. But they don't. Between fear and interest there's anxiety, then caution, then attention, then curiosity. It's all one field. We just gave names to certain points along the way — the same way we named "orange" and "green" inside a spectrum that has no actual dividing lines. Your emotional life is not a set of switches. It's a single light, refracting.

`[B-ROLL: Slow pan across a full rainbow in nature — no labels, just continuous color]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Emotions are not categories. They're a continuous spectrum.]`

## Point 2 — Emotions don't die, they transform (1:10–2:10)

Here's what changes everything. Emotions never disappear. They pass into one another. Fear doesn't vanish — it becomes caution, then attention, then understanding, then trust, then love. That's a real chain. Pride doesn't need to be destroyed — it becomes dignity without superiority. The desire to possess becomes creativity and the ability to share. Every shadow has a corresponding virtue. Anger holds courage inside it. Jealousy holds love inside it. The question is never "how do I get rid of this feeling." The question is "what does this feeling become when I stop fighting it and let it move?" The spectrum has a direction. And the direction is always toward integration — not suppression.

`[B-ROLL: Time-lapse of a sunrise — sky cycling through red, orange, gold, blue]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: Fear → caution → attention → trust → love]`

## Point 3 — The conductor, not the instrument (2:10–3:05)

So what's the endgame? In the essay I describe four levels. Level one — you're a slave to your emotions. They decide, and your mind just writes the justification afterward. Level two — you start noticing. "Oh, that's fear talking." The observer wakes up, but it's still weak. Level three — you feel the fear and you choose not to let it make the decision. That's where freedom begins. Level four — you move freely across the entire spectrum. You don't suppress anything. You use everything. Fear becomes your caution. Anger becomes your strength. Sorrow becomes your wisdom. You stop being the musician forced to play whatever melody your emotions dictate — and you become the conductor of your own orchestra. That image stayed with me. Because it means the goal was never to feel less. It was to feel everything — consciously.
