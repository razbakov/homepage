# Daily — Stop Estimating in Hours: Story Points for AI Development

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2026-03-25-stop-estimating-in-hours

## Point 1 — The ruler is broken (0:20–1:10)

Here's what's happening. You ask your AI agent how long something takes, and it says two weeks, forty hours, three sprints — whatever. And it's not lying. It learned from a world where humans wrote every line. In that world, two weeks is honest.

But you're not in that world anymore. You're in a world where scaffolding is instant, where a feature that used to take a week ships before lunch. The AI doesn't know this about itself. It estimates like a human because it learned from humans.

So hours break. A forty-hour task takes twenty minutes. A two-hour task takes three hours because the AI gets stuck on something bizarre. The variance is enormous. Hours assume stable velocity. AI development doesn't have stable velocity — it has bursts of insane productivity interrupted by weird debugging rabbit holes.

`[B-ROLL: time-lapse of a Gantt chart being drawn, then crumpling like paper]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Hours assume stable velocity. AI doesn't have that."]`

## Point 2 — Measure complexity, not time (1:10–2:10)

So what do you use instead? Story points. They measure complexity, not duration. A one-point story is simple. A five is complex. An eight — break it down.

And here's the thing — AI is actually good at judging complexity. It can look at a task, compare it to similar ones, and give you a reasonable relative size. What it can't do is convert that to hours, because its hour-calibration is from the pre-AI era.

The workflow is simple. Estimate in story points. Track your actual velocity for a week. Calibrate. Now you know — I complete X points per day with AI. Week one is chaos. By week two, patterns emerge. By week three, you can make real commitments. "This project is thirty story points. At my velocity, that's three days." That's a number you can trust.

`[B-ROLL: whiteboard with story point cards being sorted into columns]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Estimate complexity → Track velocity → Calibrate → Commit"]`

## Point 3 — T-shirt it (2:10–3:05)

In practice, I use t-shirt sizes. XS — AI does it in one shot, no review. Config change, simple CRUD. S — one round of feedback. M — multiple iterations, I'm actively involved. L — mostly human work with AI assisting. XL — that's not one task, break it down.

And the hardest part? It's not the sizing. It's talking to people who still think in hours. So I show both. "This is an M. In my experience, M takes about half a day." The t-shirt gives my team a complexity signal. The time gives managers something for their charts. Everyone gets what they need.

But whatever you do — stop nodding along when your AI says two weeks. Challenge it. Size it by complexity. Calibrate against what actually happens. Plan from data, not from inherited assumptions about human productivity.
