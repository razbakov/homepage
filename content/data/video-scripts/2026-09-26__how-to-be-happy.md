# Daily — How to be happy?

**Source:** https://razbakov.com/blog/2017-02-13-how-to-be-happy

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Eight hugs. That's the minimum. Science says you need at least eight hugs a day to be happy — and most of us aren't even close. So the real question isn't "how do I find happiness?" It's "who's touching you?"

## Point 1 — The science no one talks about (0:20–1:10)

There was this question on Quora — "Why do we need to hug and to be hugged?" — and the answer is almost embarrassingly simple. A hug floods you with oxytocin. That's the bonding hormone. It drops your blood pressure. It strengthens your immune system. It lowers cortisol — which means less stress, better sleep. This isn't poetry. This is biology. Your body is literally designed to need the touch of another human being. And we've built a world where you can go days — weeks — without it. We optimized for productivity and forgot that we're mammals. We're warm-blooded creatures who regulate each other. That's not weakness. That's architecture.

`[B-ROLL: close-up of two hands clasping, time-lapse of a sunrise]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Oxytocin. Cortisol. It's not poetry — it's biology."]`

## Point 2 — The obvious and the not-so-obvious (1:10–2:10)

Now, the obvious sources — a partner, a child, a pet, a friend. You know these. And if you have them, great. But what if you don't? Or what if those eight hugs still aren't happening? This is where it gets interesting. There are movements — literal movements — built around this. Free Hugs. You've seen the signs in city squares. A stranger opens their arms and you walk in. It sounds absurd until you do it. Then there's Contact Improvisation — sometimes called Contango. Two bodies moving together, no choreography, just weight and trust and listening through your skin. And Kizomba — this Angolan dance that's basically a conversation held entirely in an embrace. These aren't niche hobbies. These are technologies for connection that humans invented because we needed them. We always needed them.

`[B-ROLL: montage — Free Hugs sign in a plaza, Contact Improvisation dancers in a studio, Kizomba couple dancing close]`
`[TEXT ON SCREEN: "Technologies for connection."]`

## Point 3 — Why this matters now (2:10–3:05)

Here's what I keep coming back to. We talk about happiness like it's a mindset. Like you can think your way there. Read the right book. Build the right morning routine. But your nervous system doesn't care about your affirmations. It cares about contact. It cares about warmth. About pressure. About another heartbeat near yours. And if you're not getting that — if your days are screens and meetings and solo commutes — no amount of journaling is going to fill that gap. The answer might not be a new habit. It might be a dance class. It might be saying yes the next time someone opens their arms. Happiness isn't always built. Sometimes it's held.
