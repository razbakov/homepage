Life offers interesting opportunities. Some opportunities can fundamentally change everything. For instance — an offer to relocate to Germany.

Making such a decision isn't easy. To leave everything you have: your home, your familiar way of life, family, friends — it's a major step. It's much easier to embrace such changes if you've already moved before. But what's even more important — what awaits you in another country? It resembles a road fading into fog. What will happen is unknown. You can speculate and imagine, ask emigrants, read blogs. All of this is only imagination and cannot compare to what you already know. There's only one way to find out what it will actually be like — to decide. For curious and enterprising explorers, such a decision comes easier.

Visa obtained, plane ticket purchased. Departure.

## Expectations and Reality

Germany. For many, school taught us to avoid this country and language. France, Italy, Spain seemed so much better. Anywhere but Germany. The language of love in the most romantic city. Or the Colosseum, pizza, pasta. Or bullfighting, flamenco and mandarins.

Back to Germany. Those not passionate about the German language and culture only get pop culture highlights: the famous Rammstein, pornography, underground and alternative culture, Berlin graffiti and German punctuality jokes. The language is perceived as rough and harsh:

The mountains challenge everyone who passes by. In winter — ski or snowboard down them, in summer — climb to the summit and enjoy delicious Bavarian beer.

## German Language

Europeans love their own language and are not quick to learn English. Germany is no exception. Especially Bavarians. Without knowing German, you can't even open a bank account. You can find English speakers, but not always. And they love to take vacations. Sometimes at the most inconvenient moment.

They recommend watching TV and movies in German. Supposedly you'll get used to it and start remembering whole phrases and expressions. Maybe that's true. Only if you have motivation and the strength to overcome your sleepiness and exhaustion from an unfamiliar language and chaotic, unpleasant sound combinations. If there's no internal desire to learn the language, then motivation might come from outside. If your colleagues are German and your boss insists on everyone speaking only German, arguing that it's in your own interest — to learn the language and integrate. Time passes and you realize that without courses you won't get far — you need a guide through an unfamiliar language, where you feel like a drop of consciousness in an ocean of countless words, phrases, constructions and expressions. Even one course will give you an excellent foundation for self-education. If this course is not your only activity in life, you'll need to find the strength to battle the aforementioned sleepiness and exhaustion.

German comes with difficulty. It's easier to motivate yourself by surrounding yourself with Germans who converse with each other, and increasingly you want to understand what they're saying. Understanding them is not easy, because your culture and worldview are different. And the stress you experience when someone addresses you and you can't make out half the words, losing track of what to answer — such beneficial stress helps you remember important words. Not from a textbook, but truly important survival expressions. Time passes, you live surrounded by Germans, try to communicate, correspond with them, watch movies in German, listen to music and unexpectedly you learn more and more words and start to understand whole phrases. The hardest part at the beginning is understanding the context of a conversation if you're not in the know.

If you allow people around you to speak to you in English, forget your German. But limiting yourself to German dooms you to loneliness and emptiness. The more you understand, the more you become absorbed in what's happening around you.

## Friends

Friends. Sometimes it seems you live here and now and you have friends. You move to another city — new acquaintances appear. You move to another country and realize that friendship is a phantom concept. They seem to exist, but the farther apart you are, the weaker your connection becomes. Over time you stop understanding each other and drift apart.

New acquaintances in a new country grow more numerous over time, but those who could replace friends still don't appear. And where to find these new friends? There are many different social networks where you can find interest groups, sports clubs and bars.

Being an immigrant makes finding friends much more difficult. The differences in cultures, personal experience, worldview, life values and priorities can be so significant that friendship becomes almost impossible. Or it will simply be uninteresting to spend time with each other. You often meet new people at stammtisch or board game nights. Such meetings are very popular in Germany. At first you think this is a great opportunity and soon you'll have many friends. Over time you get bored with the monotony. The same questions, the same people, the same activities. And the herd nature of such events will interfere with your plans to make friends. To become close to a person, you need to isolate yourself with them, experience something unique together. But such events assume everyone talks to everyone and you have no guarantee that your conversation partner will stay with you a minute later instead of running off under the influence of new circumstances.

Want to make friends — take matters into your own hands. Start with yourself. To become an interesting person, you start exploring what you like, want to become interesting to yourself. You try something new every day. When meeting another person, use every opportunity to communicate face to face, so nothing distracts you from your conversation.

Friends will appear with time. At first it will be very lonely if you just sit at home and never go out.
